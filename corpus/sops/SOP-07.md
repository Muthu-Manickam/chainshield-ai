# SOP-07 Warehouse Damage Inspection

> Team-authored, illustrative procedure for the ChainShield demo.

## Purpose
After an earthquake, flood, fire or storm, this procedure decides whether a warehouse can keep operating, how much stock is damaged, and where inbound shipments should go.

## Roles
- **Warehouse Manager**: leads the first inspection and stock count.
- **Qualified Structural Engineer**: inspects the building structure.
- **Logistics Analyst**: redirects inbound shipments and updates inventory.
- **Operations Supervisor**: approves reopening or closure.

## Trigger
Start when an incident alert, utility failure, or staff report indicates possible damage at a site, or after any earthquake of local intensity noticeable to staff.

## Steps
1. **Safety check (within 1 hour).** Evacuate if there is fire, gas smell, flooding above floor level, or visible structural movement. Do not re-enter until it is safe.
2. **First walk-through (within 6 hours).** The Warehouse Manager checks roof, walls, racking, doors, power, fire systems and loading docks. Photograph and note damage.
3. **Classify the site.**
   - *Green*: no significant damage, operations continue.
   - *Amber*: partial damage, limited use of safe areas only.
   - *Red*: unsafe, closed until inspected.
4. **Structural inspection (within 24 hours for Amber or Red).** A qualified engineer inspects and provides written clearance or limits.
5. **Stock count (within 48 hours).** Count all stock in usable areas. Tag damaged cartons and quarantine wet or crushed goods. Compare to the system record.
6. **Redirect inbound.** For Amber or Red sites, the Analyst moves inbound shipments to an alternate warehouse and updates promised dates.
7. **Insurance notice (within 72 hours).** Notify the insurer and supply photographs and the stock count.
8. **Reopen decision.** The Supervisor approves reopening only after Green classification or written engineering clearance.

## Costs (illustrative)
Temporary storage at a third-party warehouse costs about $35 per pallet per day. Emergency inspection by an engineer costs about $2,500 per site. Stock write-offs over $10,000 need Operations Manager sign-off.

## Records
Keep the inspection checklist, photographs, count sheet and engineering letter in the incident record. These are used for insurance and in the review under SOP-08.
