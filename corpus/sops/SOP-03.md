# SOP-03 Port Disruption and Alternate Port

> Team-authored, illustrative procedure for the ChainShield demo.

## Purpose
This procedure covers the response when a seaport we depend on is closed, congested or restricted by a storm, flood, earthquake, strike or security event. It aims to decide quickly whether to wait or to divert cargo to another port.

## Roles
- **Logistics Analyst**: gathers port status and prepares the options.
- **Port Agent / Carrier Representative**: confirms berth, gate and customs conditions.
- **Operations Supervisor**: approves diversions up to $25,000.
- **Operations Manager**: approves diversions above $25,000.

## Trigger
Start when a port authority notice, carrier advisory or incident alert indicates that the port will be fully or partly closed, or when vessel waiting time exceeds 48 hours.

## Steps
1. **Assess status (within 2 hours).** Record whether the port is closed, restricted or congested, the expected reopening time, and which of our containers are on board vessels, at anchor or in the terminal.
2. **Estimate the wait.** If reopening is expected within 48 hours, normally wait and apply SOP-05 if customer orders will be late. If it is expected to take longer or is unknown, prepare an alternate port.
3. **Shortlist alternate ports (within 6 hours).** Rank by distance from the original port, available berth and yard capacity, customs clearance record, and inland road and rail links. Check inland links using SOP-01.
4. **Confirm the alternate port with the carrier (within 12 hours).** The carrier must confirm in writing that it will discharge at the alternate port, the revised arrival date, and any surcharge. Do not tell customers or trucking partners the new port until this confirmation is received.
5. **Arrange inland transport and customs.** Book trucks or rail from the alternate port, and ensure the clearing agent has the documents for the new port of entry.
6. **Approve and execute.** Obtain the approval in the table below and notify the carrier.

| Estimated extra cost of diversion | Approver |
|---|---|
| Up to $25,000 | Operations Supervisor |
| Over $25,000 | Operations Manager |

## Costs (illustrative)
A diversion usually adds about $1,200 per container in carrier surcharge and extra trucking. Container storage free time is usually 5 days, after which demurrage of about $150 per container per day applies. Compare these against the cost of waiting.

## Follow-up
Re-check the original port every 12 hours. If it reopens before cargo has been committed to the alternate port, cancel the diversion if it is cheaper to do so. Record all decisions for the incident review in SOP-08.
